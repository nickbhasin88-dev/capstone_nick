## Step 4. Episode by episode: the distribution behind the mean

An episode is a year in which DAH per person fell by at least 15% on the previous year, from a level of at least US$5 per person. The response is the change in GHES per person over that year and the next (the regression's window) per US$1 of aid lost: negative = GHES fell with the aid, positive = GHES rose (replacement), the same convention as theta. 'Adjusted' subtracts the country's usual yearly GHES change (its median outside episodes) and the year's common shock across all countries (the median change in that year net of each country's usual change; the regression's year effect), so neither the usual upward trend nor a general retrenchment year is read as a response. Per-person values are rounded to whole dollars in the IHME file, so single episodes in low-spending countries are coarse (a US$1 step on GHES of US$5-10 per person). The dollar-weighted value is the total adjusted GHES change over the episodes divided by the total aid lost: per person (the regression's implicit weighting) and weighted by population (actual dollars, so large countries dominate).

## Step 5. Summary

- The pooled -0.19 is not a property of the countries facing the cuts. Low-income countries: theta +0.01 (-0.04 to +0.06, 26 countries); the most aid-dependent third: -0.05 (-0.22 to +0.11); the 15 MOU countries: -0.09 (-0.26 to +0.08). In these groups GHES neither fell with the aid nor replaced it. The pooled co-movement comes from middle-income, low-dependence countries, where aid is small next to GHES and both move with the economy.
- Episode view: in 43% of 327 aid-fall episodes GHES per person grew less than usual (net of the year's common shock) and in 45% it grew more; the median episode shows +0.00 per US$1 of aid lost, the per-person dollar-weighted value +0.10 and the population-weighted value +0.10.
- Subsample thetas run from -1.11 to +0.34; 2 of 21 subsample CIs exclude zero (see Step 2). No group shows replacement.
- Leave-one-out: no single country moves theta by more than 0.044; it is not driven by one outlier.
- Caveats: IHME estimates (GHES and DAH are modelled series with their own uncertainty); DAH here is all health aid, not the four programme areas the dashboard models; constant 2023 US$ per person; two-year window; episodes are statistical falls in disbursements (campaign timing, Gavi tranches) as well as policy cuts, which biases the response toward zero; the panel is 'what happened', not 'what governments could do'. The dashboard default (No Government Replacement) is consistent with this evidence; it is not evidence that replacement is impossible.
