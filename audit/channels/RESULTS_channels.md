# W-7. Channel comparison: what US$1M buys through each channel (no model changes)

Generated by `audit/channels/run_channels.py`. Each channel is cut alone (`health_model.Scenario`: a donor cut on US direct channels, a donor x channel pair cut, or a channel cut for every donor) by the fraction that loses US$10M a year (half the channel's 2021-23 baseline where the baseline is below US$20M), sudden path, five years, no government response, death-rate trends on, central runs. People on service are the yearly full-cost equivalents after the 15% continuity assumption, per US$1M of aid lost per year, so channels are compared at equal money. The disease mix is the share of the lost money by disease (what the channel funds in that country); the share on service lines is the part of the money the model turns into services (the rest is prevention without units, OVC, testing beyond the kappa share, programme management and systems).

## Step 4. Reading and caveats

- The channel's disease and line mix in each country decides what a dollar buys: Gavi money is vaccines (and systems money the model treats as above-site), Global Fund money is a mix with more malaria and TB and more unspecified and systems money, US bilateral money is mostly HIV treatment and care. 'People' therefore favours malaria-heavy channels (a bednet costs US$2.20), ART patient-years favour US bilateral money, and TB courses the Global Fund.
- Cuts to multilateral channels are modelled as a uniform share of every donor's money through that channel in the country's 2021-23 mix; how the Global Fund or Gavi would actually reallocate a shortfall across countries and programmes is not modelled (the dashboard's 'Global Fund & Gavi shortfalls' preset carries the same limitation).
- Where a line is capped at the people covered or donor money exceeds the donor-funded pool, people per dollar fall with the size of the cut; the half-size check above lists those cases.
