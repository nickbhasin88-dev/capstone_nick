# I-13, I-14, I-15, I-16. Fixed

## I-13: fiscal panel shows the true gap and the gap after the government response

- **Source of the numbers.** "Can {Country} Fill the Gap?" now reads from the same cached run as the headline and the
  top summary block (`run_ctl`), including any Gradual Budget Increase money. The values come from
  `fiscal_gap_values(res)`.
- **Three values**, each an average per year over 2026-2030, with the 2030 value underneath when it differs (so not
  under the Sudden path):
  1. **Gap Before Government Steps In** = aid lost (gross) = the headline's Aid at Risk.
  2. **Filled by the Government** = replacement under the chosen response plus any gradual budget money. It shows "0"
     with "No Government Replacement selected" when that option is chosen.
  3. **Gap Left After Government Response** = 1 minus 2 = the headline's Net Aid Lost.
- **Chart.** One stacked horizontal bar per view (average, and 2030 when it differs):
  - the full gap as a light rose track;
  - the part filled, in blue;
  - the part left, in rose;
  - values printed on the bars, with a dashed "Backfill Capacity (Fiscal Space)" marker on the same axis.
  - If capacity is more than 4x the gap it would squash the bars; then it goes in a note under the chart instead.
- **Sentence:** "{Country} loses about {gap}/yr in aid; under {response} the government fills {filled}, leaving
  {left}/yr unfilled." It is followed by the fiscal-space line.
- **Context pills.** "Aid lost as % of government health spending / revenue" now uses the same average-per-year gap.
- **Checks** (`audit/checks.py`, every country x preset x response x funding path, 8,464 runs): value 3 equals the
  headline's average net loss, and 1 = 2 + 3 for both the average and 2030. **8,464 / 8,464 pass.**
- Example, Kenya, Full US Exit, linear phase-out, 25% replacement:

  | | Average per year | 2030 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Gap | $166.6M | $277.7M |
  | Filled | $24.5M | $27.1M |
  | Left | $142.1M | $250.6M |

  Fiscal space: $27.1M.

## I-15: scenarios that raise aid

- **Model.** A service line whose aid rises counts as no loss (`health_model.run_country`):
  - Rising lines are set to 0 before the money is spread over services. People losing a service, coverage drops and
    deaths are therefore never negative.
  - People losing a service is floored at 0, which only removes floating-point noise where the government replaces a
    line in full.
  - The rise is kept per disease (`gains_usd`).
- **Display.**
  - Under each affected disease in the service table: "Aid to {disease} rises by {money} in this scenario; gains aren't
    modelled."
  - The old message that counted gains as lives gained is gone.
  - The headline people total now counts every line (it used to skip lines under 0.5 people), so it equals the table.
- **Formatter.** `_pts` is now sign-aware: "−6.6 pts" for a drop, "+6.6 pts" for a rise, never "−-". Tested with a
  negative input.
- **Checks:** headline people and deaths = table totals, and no negative people or coverage drops, for every country
  x preset x response x funding path. **8,464 / 8,464 pass.**
- **Affected countries under IHME 2025 (25):**

## I-16: parameter editor

- **Constant parameters.** low == high (or low == central == high) is a constant: n copies of the central value, with
  no triangular draw. It still consumes its share of random numbers, so fixing one parameter doesn't shift the other
  parameters' draws. No parameter in `model_params.csv` is fixed today, so no number changes.
- **Invalid edits.** `validate_params` rejects low > high and central outside [low, high]. The app then shows a short
  inline message ("Not applied: …. Using the last valid values.") and keeps the previous valid table instead of
  crashing.
- **Tests:** low == high (vaccine cost, continuity, ART hazard), low > high, central outside the range, and unchanged
  draws for the other parameters.
