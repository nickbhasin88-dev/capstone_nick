# W-5. Timing: the same 2026-2030 cut, reshaped (no model changes)

Generated by `audit/timing/run_timing.py`. America First MOUs preset, US bilateral MOU schedule only (US multilateral exits off), dashboard defaults, 400 draws, seed 7, paired ranges, 15 countries. Each country's signed yearly cut fractions (1 - MOU amount / `mou_us_ref`) are reshaped keeping their sum, so the modelled aid lost over 2026-2030 is the same (the yearly cut is applied to a fixed 2021-23 baseline). Shapes use `scenarios.path_shape`. 'Delayed one year, same total' moves every year's cut one year later and scales the four remaining years up; 'shifted' moves them later without scaling (the 2030 cut falls outside the window). People on service are summed over 2026-2030 (ART: patient-years off treatment over the five years). Deaths 2026-30 and deaths set in motion for 2031-35 are secondary; the 'committed' column adds them, which matters here because a later cut moves deaths past 2030.

## Step 4. Mechanism and caveats

- A patient-year off ART in year 1 of a cut carries a 1.3% excess death hazard, rising to 6-7% by years 5-7 off treatment (`health_model.ART_HAZARD`), and the cohort also transmits HIV while off treatment; TB cases spread 1-2 years after an untreated year; a missed birth cohort's vaccine-preventable deaths arrive over the following years. So the same patient-years lost later in the window produce fewer deaths inside 2026-2030 and more after it, and a delay shortens the time cohorts spend at the high end of the hazard curve before funding returns in 2031.
- People on service: because the yearly cut is applied to a fixed baseline, the sum of patient-years lost over the window is the same for every equal-total shape unless a line is capped at the people covered; differences in the people columns are therefore cap and cohort effects only, and the honest headline for timing is the deaths column, not coverage.
- Model boundary: funding is assumed back from 2031 and people off ART return at the `art_reengage` rate, and vaccine deaths beyond 2035 are not counted, so a back-loaded or delayed cut looks cheaper partly because part of its harm falls outside the model's window. Compare the 'set in motion' column, not the 2026-30 column alone.
